Why nobody has fixed it

Everyone knows the fix is a cold room and a cold truck. Nobody builds them, because the usual way takes two to three years. A refrigerated warehouse is a 12 to 30 month build costing millions before a crate goes in, and it needs mains power, which means waiting 26 to 40 weeks for a transformer.

So the money never gets spent, and the food keeps rotting.

A usual cold warehouse12 to 30 months
A CryoChain siteAbout 2 months

We do not build a warehouse, and we never file for a new power connection. That is what takes the schedule from years to months.

What is on a site

Just under an acre, and the same drawing at every site. Nothing on it is a building we have to construct.

  • Two 40 ft refrigerated containers. This is the cold store. They arrive on a truck ready to plug in, and we add space by adding a container.
  • Two refrigerated plug in hybrid trucks that collect at the farm and deliver to the buyer, temperature logged the whole way.
  • A 20 ft container office, with the truck chargers on its wall.
  • Solar panels on one canopy over the trucks, and a battery that carries the cold store through a full day without power.

Why the power matters

The site makes and stores its own electricity, so it never joins the queue for the power company and can sit far out on a farm corridor with no grid at all.

Because the design is frozen, the second site is a repeat order rather than a new design: the same parts, the same permit, and a crew that has done it before.

What we are not doing

There is a large business in doing this one thing properly, and doing it first.

  • No big warehouse
  • No grid application
  • No software sold to farmers
  • Nothing outside moving cold food
