How we help, in three short stories

One cold network, three kinds of customer.

Farmers

From selling in a hurry to selling on order

A farmer picks his tomatoes and has only a few days to sell them before they turn. Everyone harvested the same week, the market floods, and he takes whatever he is offered.

With CryoChain:

  • He gets an order with the price and pickup day before he picks.
  • Our truck collects at his farm gate, and his crop is cold within hours.
  • He is paid by mobile money within 24 hours, and can take part of it as fertilizer.

Half the food Ghana grows never gets eaten.

A farmer picks his tomatoes on Tuesday morning. By the afternoon they are sitting in the sun at 32 degrees. He has no cold room, and the truck that comes has no cold box. At the same time, a hotel in Accra cannot find anyone who will promise good tomatoes at a price, a grade and a date.

Week oneAbout GH₵ 600
Week twoAbout GH₵ 300
Week threeGH₵ 150, or thrown away

The tomatoes did not get worse. He ran out of time. Across Ghana, 30 to 50 percent of fruit and vegetables are lost after picking, worth about $1.9 billion US dollars a year.

From the order to the delivery

Every pickup fills a confirmed order, so no food is picked without somewhere to go.

  1. Buyer orders

    A hotel, grocer, camp or buyer abroad places and pays for an order in the app.

  2. We find the farmers

    We share the order across several farmers. Each one knows the price and pickup day in advance.

  3. Weighed at the farm

    Our agent weighs and grades each lot at the farm gate, on a phone that works with no signal.

  4. Cold within hours

    The crop goes straight into a cold truck within hours of picking, not at the end of the day.

  5. Farmer paid

    Mobile money within 24 hours. Part can be taken as fertilizer if the farmer chooses.

  6. Delivered cold

    The truck logs its temperature all the way. The buyer signs for the order on arrival.

One ton of yams, start to finish

An example of how one order moves, from an exporter's order to their packhouse.

  1. The exporter orders

    An exporter orders one ton of yams for Thursday in the app.

  2. Four farmers fill it

    We share it across four farmers, 250 kg each. Each farmer knows the price and pickup day in advance.

  3. One truck collects

    Our truck stops at every farm gate. Each lot is weighed, graded and cooled within hours.

  4. Everyone is paid and served

    Each farmer is paid by mobile money within 24 hours. On Thursday the yams arrive cold at the exporter's packhouse, with the temperature record export buyers ask for.

Every load keeps a record

Every lot is logged at the farm: the farmer, the crop, the grade, the weight, the time it was picked, the time it went into the cold, and a photo. Trucks and cold containers report their temperature all the time, so a problem is caught while the load can still be saved.

  • Farmers see their weight, grade and payment.
  • Buyers see which farm their food came from.
  • Truck customers get the temperature record for their booking.

One app runs it

Farmers see their orders and payments. Buyers order and follow their delivery. Businesses book a cold truck by the hour or the day. Farmers without a smartphone get the same news by text message, and it all keeps working when the signal drops.
