## The Truck Is Part of the Solution

Imagine a truck leaving a farm loaded with fresh tomatoes. The farmer has spent months growing them, and a buyer is waiting at the other end. But between the farm and the market, there are costs to consider: fuel, refrigeration, distance and time.

Now imagine doing this every day, across multiple farms and markets.

This is the reality of food transportation, and it is one of the challenges CryoChain is working to address.

Our decision to explore electric vehicles (EVs) comes from a simple question: **How can we move food more efficiently without making transportation an even bigger burden?**

## Why Keep Depending on Diesel?

Fuel is a major consideration for any transport business. Prices change, operating costs add up, and refrigerated trucks have additional energy needs because keeping food cool requires power even while the vehicle is on the road.

For a business building a network of refrigerated trucks, these costs matter. They affect how efficiently vehicles can operate and how affordable it is to move food between farms, storage facilities and buyers.

Electric vehicles offer another way forward. Instead of relying entirely on conventional fuels to power the vehicle, they use electricity. Depending on local electricity prices, vehicle performance and daily routes, this can offer opportunities to reduce energy and maintenance costs.

For CryoChain, that possibility makes EVs worth serious consideration as we plan the future of our fleet.

## But We Are Not Just Looking at the Engine

A refrigerated truck does more than carry goods. It must help maintain the right temperature for products that can lose their quality when exposed to unsuitable conditions.

That makes our approach different from simply choosing an electric vehicle for ordinary deliveries.

We need to consider the vehicle, the refrigeration system, the distance travelled, the weight carried and where charging will be available. We must also account for the energy needed to keep products cool during loading, transportation and delivery.

These details matter because an electric truck is only useful if it can do the job reliably.

Our focus is on exploring solutions that combine transportation and refrigeration in a way that suits the realities of moving agricultural products across Ghana.

## What Happens When We Connect EVs to Clean Energy?

This is where the opportunity becomes even more interesting.

CryoChain's vision extends beyond trucks. We are working towards a connected cold chain system that includes storage hubs, refrigerated transportation and energy solutions.

Electric vehicles could work alongside solar power, battery energy storage and charging infrastructure to support this system. For example, a suitably designed cold chain hub could use solar energy and battery storage to help power its operations and support vehicle charging.

The practical benefits would depend on the system's design, energy supply and operating costs. But the opportunity is clear: transportation and cold storage should not be planned as separate problems when they are part of the same supply chain.

## What Does This Mean for Ghana's Farmers?

For farmers, the technology itself is not the final goal. What matters is whether it helps them get their produce to buyers in good condition, reach more markets and reduce avoidable losses.

A more efficient transportation network could help connect farms to supermarkets, restaurants, schools and food businesses. When combined with suitable cold storage and dependable logistics, it could also help reduce the pressure to sell perishable produce immediately after harvest.

That is the bigger picture behind our interest in electric vehicles.

We want to build infrastructure that responds to the needs of agriculture, rather than introduce technology simply because it is new.

## Choosing What Comes Next

Electric vehicles are not without challenges. Charging infrastructure, vehicle range, purchase costs and refrigeration demands all need careful consideration, particularly when operating across different parts of Ghana.

But these challenges are reasons to plan properly, not reasons to ignore the opportunities.

At CryoChain, our ambition is to rethink how food moves from the farm to the final buyer. Electric vehicles are one of the technologies we are exploring to help make that journey more efficient and sustainable.

**Because the future of food is not just about growing more. It is about moving what we grow smarter, keeping it fresh and making sure more of it reaches the people who need it.**
