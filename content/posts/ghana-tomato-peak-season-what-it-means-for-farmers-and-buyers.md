## 1. When the Markets Are Full, Who Really Benefits?

Walk through some of Ghana's major markets during the tomato peak season, and you will find baskets and boxes filled with fresh tomatoes. Traders are busy, supplies are coming in from farming communities, and buyers have plenty to choose from.

For consumers, this may seem like the perfect time to buy. More tomatoes in the market often mean lower prices. But for the farmers who spent months growing them, the situation can be very different.

When large quantities of tomatoes arrive at the market at the same time, farmers and traders can struggle to sell everything before the produce begins to spoil. Prices fall, buyers become selective, and farmers may have little choice but to accept whatever price is offered.

The question is: why should a good harvest become a financial problem?

![Tomato Glut](/images/uploads/golden-hour-tomato-harvest-market.png "Tomato Glut")

## 2. The Hidden Cost of a Tomato Glut

Tomato production in Ghana is seasonal. During periods of high production, particularly in the rainy season, large quantities enter the market. However, when production drops, supplies become limited and prices can rise significantly.

A study published in *Sustainability* found that low farm-gate prices were a major marketing challenge for Ghanaian tomato farmers. When many farmers harvest at the same time, oversupply reduces their bargaining power. Some farmers even abandon their fields when prices become too low to justify harvesting.

The Ghana News Agency also reported in August 2023 that tomato prices had dropped in Accra markets because of a seasonal glut. Traders explained that they sometimes sold tomatoes at the same price they had purchased them simply to avoid losing everything when the produce went bad.

This shows that the problem affects more than farmers. Traders also face financial risks, while consumers experience a different challenge when supply becomes limited.

## 3. What Happens When the Season Changes?

When local tomato production declines, the situation often reverses.

Buyers who previously enjoyed lower prices begin facing higher costs. Market traders have fewer local supplies to purchase, and Ghana may increasingly depend on tomatoes from other producing countries to meet demand.

For households, tomatoes are an important part of everyday cooking. For restaurants, food vendors and businesses, changes in tomato prices can affect their operating costs.

This creates an unusual situation: during peak season, there can be more tomatoes than the market can absorb, yet during other periods, buyers struggle to find affordable supplies.

Ghana does not simply need to grow more tomatoes. It needs better ways to manage the tomatoes it already produces.

## 4. The Missing Link: Storage, Processing and Transportation

One of the biggest problems is the limited infrastructure available to preserve fresh produce after harvesting.

Tomatoes are highly perishable. Poor handling, delays in transportation and unsuitable storage conditions can cause them to rot or become damaged before reaching consumers.

Research on Ghana's tomato value chain has identified unreliable markets, poor transportation and inadequate storage as important causes of post-harvest losses.

Better storage can help manage short-term supply pressures, while processing can turn surplus tomatoes into products such as paste, puree and canned tomatoes. Refrigerated transportation can also help maintain suitable conditions as produce moves from farms to buyers.

These solutions can help reduce waste, although they must be supported by suitable equipment, reliable energy, market demand and effective distribution systems.

Farmers should be able to look at a successful harvest as an opportunity, not a reason to worry about where they will sell their produce.
